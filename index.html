About

My research examines how individuals and organizations make decisions under risk and uncertainty.

I am a Professor of Economics in the Department of Economics, Finance & Legal Studies at the University of Alabama. Prior to joining the University of Alabama, I held faculty appointments at the University of Maryland (College Park), the University of Nevada (Reno), the University of Exeter, and the University of Warwick. I have also been a Visiting Scholar at CY Cergy Paris and the International Monetary Fund, and have consulted for the United Nations Development Programme and several other international organizations. I hold a Ph.D. in Economics from Iowa State University.

My work spans decision theory, behavioral economics, applied game theory, and environmental and resource economics. More recently, it has explored the role of unforeseen contingencies in economic decision-making, random and incomplete preferences, multi-layer ambiguity, ambiguity in strategic environments, information exchange in bilateral strategic interactions, and organizational responses to unexpected shocks.

My research has been published in leading journals across economics, management, and psychology, including the Journal of the European Economic Association, Management Science, Trends in Cognitive Sciences, Academy of Management Review, Economic Theory, Psychological Review, the Journal of Risk and Uncertainty, the Journal of Environmental Economics and Management, Risk Analysis, the American Journal of Agricultural Economics, and the International Journal of Industrial Organization. This interdisciplinary work bridges economics, cognitive science, and management, contributing to a deeper understanding of a range of behavioral phenomena.
