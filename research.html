Research

Ambiguity and decision under uncertainty

Much of my work concerns choice when a single probability distribution is not available, and preferences are represented instead by sets of priors, capacities, or incomplete orderings. This includes axiomatic foundations for models of ambiguity attitude, the geometry of imprecision, elicitation of beliefs and of the core of a capacity, and the behaviour of ambiguity-sensitive agents in markets and contests.

Unforeseen contingencies and belief revision

A more recent strand asks what happens when the state space itself is incomplete — when agents become aware of contingencies they had not previously conceived. This raises questions about how beliefs should be revised on the discovery of a new event, how contracts perform when parties are unaware of relevant contingencies, and what can be measured experimentally.

Virtual bargaining and social interaction

With Nick Chater and Hossam Zeitoun, I have developed the idea that people coordinate by asking what agreement they would reach if they could bargain explicitly, and then acting on it. The approach has been applied to tacit collusion, team production, moral reasoning, and the emergence of unwritten social rules.

Contract design, regulation and the environment

An earlier and continuing line of work studies incentive contracts and regulatory instruments when information is asymmetric and the regulator faces scientific uncertainty — food safety, biosecurity, rangeland management, and climate policy among them.
